# SRVCABLE: research brief (as of 2026-09-23)

## Snapshot
Sarvottam Cables Ltd manufactures power cables, wires, and conductors [S3]. In Q1 FY27, the company reported revenue from operations of ₹1,248 crore (up 18.0% year-on-year) and profit after tax of ₹82 crore (up 5.9% year-on-year) [S3]. The order book stood at ₹3,900 crore as of 30 June 2026, driven by demand from power transmission and data centres [S2, S3].

## Bull case
- **Expanding order book and data-centre exposure:** The order book increased 23.8% year-on-year to ₹3,900 crore [S3], with data-centre cables currently contributing about 9% of domestic revenue and management projecting this share could double in two years [S2]. *Why it matters: Strong demand provides medium-term revenue visibility in high-growth infrastructure segments.*
- **Upcoming capacity expansion:** The ₹620 crore Bharuch plant is roughly 70% funded/spent and scheduled for commercial production in Q3 FY27, adding approximately 30% to cable capacity [S2, S3]. *Why it matters: The expansion should unlock additional volume growth in the second half of FY27 while maintaining a conservative net debt to equity ratio of 0.35 times [S2].*
- **Growing export share:** Exports rose to 21% of revenue in Q1 FY27 from 16% in Q1 FY26 [S3]. *Why it matters: Greater geographic diversification helps cushion the company against domestic cyclical slowdowns.*
- **Substantial decline in promoter pledge:** Pledged shares (promoter shares committed as collateral for loans) dropped to 4.1% of promoter holdings in June 2026, down from 11.5% in June 2025 [S7] and 35% in March 2024 [S4]. *Why it matters: A sharp reduction in pledged shares significantly lowers the risk of forced selling by lenders during market downturns.*

## Bear case
- **Input cost pressure and margin contraction:** EBITDA margin (operating profit as a percentage of sales) contracted by 170 basis points year-on-year to 11.2% due to a nearly 14% rise in raw material copper costs during Q1 FY27 [S1, S3]. *Why it matters: Although management states two-thirds of contracts have price-variation clauses, they adjust with a 1-to-2 quarter lag, leaving near-term profitability exposed to raw material inflation [S2].*
- **Lengthening working capital cycle:** Receivable days (how long customers take to pay) stretched from 78 to 96 days over the past year due to slower disbursements from two large state utility clients [S2]. *Why it matters: Delayed customer collections tie up cash in working capital and elevate operational risk.*
- **Substantial GST tax demand:** The company received an order on 2 September 2026 demanding ₹46.3 crore in tax, interest, and penalties regarding input tax credit claims from FY21 and FY22 [S6]. *Why it matters: Super Investing notes this demand equals approximately 56% of Q1 FY27 net profit (₹82 crore [S3]), creating a material earnings risk if an appeal fails.*

## Open questions
- **Revenue discrepancy:** News coverage reported Q1 FY27 revenue as ₹1,428 crore (+35% YoY) [S1], whereas the company's official filing states ₹1,248 crore (+18.0% YoY) [S3]. We prioritize the official filing [S3], but the reason for the news outlet's higher figure is unexplained.
- **Arithmetic mismatch on profit growth:** The Q1 FY27 release lists profit after tax growth of +5.9% (₹82 crore vs ₹77 crore in Q1 FY26) [S3]. Direct calculation on these reported figures yields +6.5%, likely indicating underlying rounding differences in the reported numbers.
- **Utility receivable recovery:** While management stated no defaults have occurred [S2], the documents do not indicate when payments from the two delayed state utilities are expected to normalize.
- **Tax dispute merits:** Management asserts it has a "strong case on merits" and expects "no material impact" [S6], but legal appeals carry uncertain timelines and outcomes.
